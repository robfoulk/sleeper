## Platform

web

## Users

The eight owners of a private eight-team fantasy football keeper league, founded
2024, plus the friends and family they share links with. They arrive on a phone,
usually on a Tuesday after the week's games have settled, to read what happened
and argue about it. A smaller second job is lookup: settling a claim about a
record, a head-to-head series, or who won a given year.

Nobody here is a stranger to the league. Every visitor already knows the eight
owners and most of the running jokes, so the site never has to explain what the
league is — only what happened in it.

## Product Purpose

A permanent, public home for the league's history and its current season. Two
seasons of recaps, season reviews, awards, and charts currently sit as loose
markdown and PDFs that nobody reads. The site turns that archive into something
worth visiting: browsable, linkable, and durable enough that a claim about 2024
can be settled in one tap in 2030.

Success is that the weekly recap gets read on a phone the day it publishes, and
that the record book gets opened during arguments.

## Positioning

The league already has a working generation pipeline that no ordinary league
site has: recaps are written by an AI writer against deterministic, verified
league data, proofread by a second model, and shipped with JSON sidecars. The
differentiator is that every number on this site is traceable to a sidecar
rather than to a writer's memory — the prose entertains, the data adjudicates.

## Operating Context

- Rebuilt weekly through the season. A recap is generated by CLI, reviewed, and
  committed; the site rebuilds and deploys itself from that commit.
- Read overwhelmingly on phones, in short sittings, immediately after the week
  resolves.
- Published to GitHub Pages from this repository.
- The 2026 season kicks off around September 10, 2026.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Content is markdown in `recaps/{season}/`, with JSON sidecars per season:
  `season-aggregate`, `season-awards`, `season-outcome`, `power-history`,
  `team-name-history`, `manifest`.
- `site/src/data/league.json` is the single generated data layer: seasons,
  standings, weekly results, franchises, owners, all-time records, a full
  head-to-head matrix, and an article index.
- Charts exist as committed SVGs in `recaps/charts/`.
- **Privacy is a hard build-time requirement, not a preference.** No surnames,
  no Sleeper usernames, and no personal-relationship framing may appear in any
  published output. The league renders as "The League". This is enforced by
  `PrivacyGuardTests`, which scans `recaps/**` and `site/**` and fails the
  build on a violation.
- Franchise-versus-owner continuity is already decided in the data layer: a
  franchise is a roster slot and survives an ownership change; an owner keeps
  only the seasons he played. Roster 8 passed from a departing owner to Travon
  for 2026, and the site must show that as a handoff, not as inheritance.
- Scoring is non-PPR with a deep 30-slot roster. Player value on this site
  cannot be reasoned about with standard PPR assumptions.
- 2026 is unplayed. Projections rest on 2025 production only.

## Brand Commitments

- The league is called **"The League"**. The former name never appears.
- **First names only, everywhere** — prose, headings, tables, chart labels, page
  titles, meta tags, and alt text.
- Rivalries are earned from results — head-to-head, eliminations, rematches,
  standings stakes — never from personal or family relationships between owners.
- Voice: one beat reporter covering the whole league for the whole week.
  Specific, confident, a little dry, willing to needle an owner over a decision
  but never over a person.
- Team names are the league's running joke and its mood ring; owners rename
  constantly and a rename usually betrays how the season is going. The site must
  treat team-name history as content, not noise.

## Evidence on Hand

Real and verified:

- `recaps/2024/` and `recaps/2025/` — 18 markdown articles each plus six JSON
  sidecars each.
- `recaps/2026/export.json` — the 2026 draft board, 25 keepers verified against
  2025 end-of-season rosters, rosters, schedule, and prior-season production.
- `site/src/data/league.json` — the merged data layer.
- `recaps/charts/*.svg` — eight committed charts.

Absent, and not to be fabricated: any 2026 results, any live news or injury
reporting, any sourced ADP (Sleeper's `search_rank` is a market proxy only), and
any statement about an owner's personal life.

## Product Principles

1. **Numbers come from data; the model only explains and entertains.** Scores,
   standings, and records render from JSON, never from parsed prose.
2. **Privacy is enforced, not remembered.** A build that would leak fails.
3. **The archive is the product.** Every season, article, and chart stays
   reachable and linkable forever.
4. **Phone first.** A score table that is unreadable on a phone has failed,
   however good it looks on a laptop.
5. **Earned, not inherited.** Rivalries come from results, and records follow
   the person who earned them.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Read primarily on phones in short sittings. Dense score and standings tables are
the core content, so they must stay legible and navigable at small widths and
with a screen reader; charts need text equivalents because they carry real
information rather than decoration.
