# 2026 Season Preview — The League

_Power tiers below are computed, not felt. Each team's projection fills this league's actual
starting lineup — two quarterbacks, four running backs, four receivers, two tight ends, two
flexes, two kickers, two defenses — from its current roster, using each player's 2025 points
per game under this league's own scoring. Players with no 2025 production count as zero and are
reported separately rather than guessed at. It measures what a roster has already proven, which
is the only thing about 2026 that can be measured at all._

## The one-line version

Rob spent the winter buying picks and is now 24 points a week clear of the field. Behind him,
four teams project within a point and a half of each other. And Dave, who scored more
regular-season points than anyone in the league last year, comes out last.

## What changed since the draft

Rosters are not draft boards. Reconciling draft night against the Week 1 rosters, five players
changed owners in three apparent trades, eleven draft-night players are gone from league
rosters entirely, and eleven players nobody drafted have taken their places. Two of the trades
reshaped a projection.

- **Jake sent Trey McBride and Quentin Johnston to Travon for Ashton Jeanty.** Travon got the
  tight end and the receiver depth he needed; Jake got a fifth running back and gave up his only
  high-end tight end. It is the single biggest reason Jake projects seventh.
- **Dave sent Jaxon Smith-Njigba to Devin.** Dave's best keeper — a ninth-round price on a
  14.2-point receiver — now leads Devin's receiving corps. Devin projects second partly on the
  strength of it.
- **Jake sent Keenan Allen to Michael.**

Everything below reflects rosters as they stand, not as they were drafted.

## Tier I — Rob

**Unstoppable Farce, 213.6.** He led this league in Week 1 last year, fell from first to
seventh, finished 6-9, drafted third, and then went and did something about it. Seven acquired
picks, six late picks spent to get them, three of the last six selections in round two.

The lineup that came out of it is the deepest in the league at nearly every position. Trevor
Lawrence and Jalen Hurts at 19.9 and 18.7. A starting backfield of De'Von Achane, Derrick
Henry, Josh Jacobs and Jaylen Warren that projects **56.0** between them — and then Rhamondre
Stevenson and Zach Charbonnet in the flexes at 10.9 and 10.1, which is a fifth and sixth
running back most teams would start. Four receivers between 10.6 and 11.8: no star, no hole.

And a bench that projects **70.7** across six players, 15.5 clear of the next-deepest team.
Over a fifteen-week regular season, that is its own kind of advantage.

The one soft spot is the second tight end, where Colston Loveland at 6.7 drags the slot down
behind Brock Bowers.

Anything short of a playoff berth here would be a genuine failure.

## Tier II — the four-way tie

These four project inside 1.4 points of one another. That is smaller than the swing of a single
quarterback having a bad afternoon, so what follows is about shape, not rank.

**Devin — Amon Another level, 189.4.** Projects second, and the interesting part is who isn't in
the lineup. Justin Jefferson, kept at a third-round price, returned 6.9 points a game in 2025
and does not crack Devin's starting four receivers — Jaxon Smith-Njigba, Amon-Ra St. Brown, Tee
Higgins and Marvin Harrison get there first. Smith-Njigba was Dave's keeper on draft night;
Devin went and got him. Bijan Robinson at 1.02 and Jaxson Dart at an eighteenth-round keeper
price are both excellent. This roster projects second *without* its most expensive keeper
contributing anything. If Jefferson is himself again, there is another gear here that the table
cannot see.

**Travon — Von, 188.2.** The new owner of the eighth roster slot, and the only person in the
league who built from nothing: no keepers, thirty picks, and a third-place projection. Patrick
Mahomes and Brock Purdy give him the second-best quarterback pair in the league at 40.0
combined, for the price of a fifth and an eleventh. Jonathan Taylor at 1.04 projects 18.6, the
most productive running back in the league by 2025 scoring. Then he flipped Ashton Jeanty — backfield depth
behind Taylor — to the champion for Trey McBride and Quentin Johnston, which is a first-year
owner reading his own roster correctly.

The cost is behind Taylor. Quinshon Judkins, David Montgomery and Rachaad White fill out a
backfield that projects 43.4 across four starters — third-thinnest in the league. The
franchise he bought went 4-13 in 2025 under its previous owner and forfeited a keeper for it;
Travon inherited the roster and none of the record. Welcome.

**Brian — Tri-Lams, 188.0.** Went 13-2, the best regular-season record in the two-season
archive, and lost in the semifinal to a 7-8 team. He returns Christian McCaffrey at 18.5 and
the league's most expensive-looking keeper class. The receivers are the problem: **eleven**
rostered and not one projecting above Nico Collins at 10.3. So is the bench, which projects
40.5 — second-worst in the league. Brian has the best regular season on record and nothing to
show for it, which is the kind of grievance that fuels a year.

**Eric — Sanders Boutte on Sunday, 187.9.** Won the consolation final, took the 1.01, and used
it on Jahmyr Gibbs. The backfield is real — Gibbs, Chase Brown, Rico Dowdle, RJ Harvey at 49.4
across four. The quarterbacks are not: Joe Burrow and Jayden Daniels project **33.1** combined,
the lowest pair in the league, in a format that starts two of them every week. Eric climbed
from sixth to fourth in the 2025 power ranks. The path to a louder climb runs through the one
position he did not address.

## Tier III — the near miss

**Michael — Cripple, 184.5.** Runner-up in 2025 after starting the year ranked eighth, and the
owner of a quarterback room — Drake Maye at 20.6 and Lamar Jackson at 16.5 — that cost him a
fifteenth-round keeper and a second-round pick. He also has the fewest unproven players on any
roster, just two, so this projection is better grounded than most.

That is the good news. The backfield projects **39.3** across four starters, last in the league,
with running backs three and four at 6.4 apiece. Michael reached the final last year on an 8-7
regular season. He is going to have to do something like that again.

**Jake — Performance Anxiety, 180.6.** Two-time defending champion, projecting seventh, and
nobody should read that as bad news. He won the 2025 title from a 7-8 regular season, beat the
13-2 team in the semifinal, and won the final by 48. Puka Nacua at a fourteenth-round keeper
price is one of the two best-value contracts in the league.

The projection dislikes him for two specific reasons: he punted quarterback, and he traded his
only high-end tight end to Travon. His tight ends now project **11.8** combined, worst in the
league. Both are real. Neither has stopped him yet.

## Tier IV — Dave

**Early and out of hope, 175.2.** The most top-heavy roster in the league, and the one the
projection is least confident about.

Josh Allen and Matthew Stafford project **43.4** between them, the best quarterback pair in the
league by 3.4, and Stafford cost only a fifteenth-round keeper slot. Counting both flexes, his
projected lineup gets **54.4** from six receivers — more receiver production than anyone else's
— and that is *after* trading Jaxon Smith-Njigba, his best keeper, to Devin.

Then it stops. His four starting running backs project **40.6** combined, and he rosters
**nine** players with no 2025 production at all — the most in the league by four. Jeremiyah
Love, taken in round three, is one of them. A projection that scores nine players as zero is not
really a projection of this team; it is a projection of the two-thirds of it that has played.

Which matters, because Dave scored **2,823.1** regular-season points in 2025 — the most in the
league, by two points over Brian — and finished fourth. This roster has the same signature:
enormous at the top, unreadable underneath.

## What is actually unknown

Three things should temper all of the above.

**2026 has not been played.** These projections rest entirely on 2025 production. A player who
changed teams, changed roles, or is entering his second year is weakly grounded here, and the
model has no way to know it.

**Players without a 2025 line count as zero.** Thirty-five rostered players across the league
have no 2025 line at
all. Dave has nine and Brian has five. Each one is a projection of zero that could be a
starter, and that uncertainty runs hardest against the teams that drafted youngest.

**This is a snapshot, not the news.** The roster data was captured on September 5, 2026.
Injuries, depth-chart changes, and suspensions after that date are invisible to everything
written here. Check the actual news before you set a lineup.

## Five predictions, and you can check every one

Falsifiable on purpose. No hedging, no "should contend."

1. **Rob finishes the regular season first in points scored.** He projects 24.2 points a week
   clear of second. If the projection means anything at all, it means this.
2. **Dave makes the championship bracket anyway.** Against his own last-place projection —
   because nine unproven players is a measurement failure, not a talent failure, and he led the
   league in scoring last season.
3. **Eric misses the championship bracket.** The lowest quarterback pair in the league, in a
   two-quarterback format, over fifteen weeks.
4. **Travon finishes ahead of at least three teams that entered with four keepers.** He projects
   third with zero keepers. Four owners kept the maximum.
5. **Jake does not win a third straight title.** He projects seventh, his tight ends are the
   worst in the league, and both of his championships came from a field that has now spent a
   winter watching him do it. Somebody stops it.
