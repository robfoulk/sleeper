# Week 1 Final: Three at the Top, Nobody Comfortable

_The League, 2026. Final Week 1 results; reporting snapshot September 15, 2026, 15:17 UTC (11:17 a.m. Eastern)._

Devin won the week. Rob nearly stole the headline. Brian nearly stole it from both of them without a single starter reaching 20 points. After all the football was finished, the top three were separated by **2.26 points**: 210.80, 208.88, 208.54. An entire summer of roster construction, reduced to the fantasy equivalent of checking the receipt for loose change.

Dave also cleared 200. None of the four losing teams reached that mark, and none of the actual matchups finished within 24 points. The suspense was in the scoring race, not a last-second fantasy comeback. Week 2 gets the hint: Rob and Devin play each other next.

## Four Games, Four Different Problems

### Devin 210.80, Eric 163.02: Another Level, by a Very Small Amount

The team name got its opening-week endorsement. Devin's Amon Another level took the high score with Jaxson Dart supplying 26.60, Bijan Robinson 23.30, Justin Jefferson 23.20, and Dallas Goedert 19.70. That is not one player dragging a lineup across the finish line. That is production arriving from several directions while the opponent tries to decide which leak to plug first.

Eric had the best individual score in this matchup: Jahmyr Gibbs' 28.60. He also had Jayden Daniels at 17.66 and Joe Burrow at 14.16. The problem was the rest of the comparison. Having the biggest performance in one slot does not purchase immunity from the other seventeen, and Sanders Boutte on Sunday finished 47.78 points behind.

Devin gets the opening-week crown, but not much elbow room. His lead over Rob was only 1.92. For Eric, the useful distinction is between an ugly result and a hopeless roster. Gibbs already delivered; Week 2 needs more help, not a ceremonial demolition of the team.

### Rob 208.88, Jake 150.78: Performance Review

Caleb Williams scored 37.26. Derrick Henry added 34.30. Kenneth Walker supplied 31.10. Those three alone accounted for **102.66**, nearly half of Unstoppable Farce's total. Jalen Hurts' 24.72 and Pittsburgh's 20.00 gave Rob enough support to make the week's largest winning margin, 58.10, feel entirely earned.

Jake's new Performance Anxiety branding was offered an unreasonably quick field test. Ashton Jeanty did his part with 26.70, Javonte Williams added 19.20, and Ladd McConkey contributed 14.20. The lineup still finished eighth. There is a difference between acquiring a star running back and acquiring eighteen functioning scoring slots; the rest of the roster has been invited to demonstrate that difference less vividly next week.

Rob also left Jalen Coker's 25.80 on the bench while starting Ja'Marr Chase for 1.20. A same-position swap would have added 24.60, but that is hindsight, not proof the original decision was unreasonable. Winning by 58.10 while finding something to complain about is a very efficient use of a Sunday.

### Brian 208.54, Michael 169.18: The Entire Lineup Was the Star

Tri-Lams reached 208.54 with no starter scoring 20. Isaiah Likely led at 19.80, Jordan Love posted 19.48, Evan McPherson kicked in 19.00, and DJ Moore added 16.00. Brian's two starting defenses, Las Vegas and Seattle, combined for another 28.00. Not every win needs a 40-point centerpiece. Sometimes the uncomfortable answer is that the other team kept finding points everywhere.

Michael got 24.96 from Lamar Jackson, 20.70 from Chuba Hubbard, and 17.80 from Breece Hall. Parker Washington's 14.30 was useful, too. Broken leg broken team still lost by 39.36. The rename from Disappointment did not erase the old department's responsibilities.

One ownership detail matters here: **A.J. Brown's 2.60 remains in Michael's Week 1 locked lineup**, even though Brown belongs to Rob now. Historical scoring belongs to the lineup that earned it. The trade changes the next chapter, not this box score.

### Dave 201.06, Travon 176.56: Early, but Not Out of Points

Dave's Early and out of hope opened with 201.06, a fairly aggressive rebuttal of its own name. Josh Allen scored 35.66, D'Andre Swift 31.40, and Christian Watson 26.70. That trio supplied **93.76**. Allen and Swift attracted the attention in the early draft of the story; Watson deserves a full chair at the table in the final one.

Travon had the week's strongest losing score at 176.56. David Montgomery's 25.90, Jonathan Taylor's 22.10, Patrick Mahomes' 21.66, and Brock Purdy's 21.10 made the top of the lineup competitive. The gap was lower down: Terry McLaurin had 1.40, Quentin Johnston 1.70, and Jordan Addison 0.00.

The 24.50-point defeat was the closest game of the week. That tells you more about Week 1's uneven matchups than it does about a nail-biter. Travon leaves 0-1, but he also outscored the other three losing teams. Next comes Jake, and one of these teams has a chance to make its first week look like a detour.

## The Trade Desk

**September 12: Rob buys a delayed arrival.** Rob received A.J. Brown and Michael's 2027 eighth-round pick. Michael received Isaac TeSlaa and Rob's 2027 fourth-round pick. No FAAB changed hands. The [Patriots announced Brown's IR placement that same date](https://www.patriots.com/news/analysis-patriots-place-wr-a-j-brown-on-injured-reserve-sign-dt-daquan-jones), after he left the September 9 opener. This is a future-production bet for Rob, not immediate Week 2 reinforcement. Michael gets a younger receiver and moves from an eighth to a fourth in the exchanged picks. Neither side's private reasoning is in the transaction record.

Two **August 31** deals also deserve their first regular-season check-in, not a false label as this week's news:

- **Devin received** Jaxon Smith-Njigba plus Dave's 2027 twelfth- and eighteenth-round picks. **Dave received** Chris Bell plus Devin's 2027 second- and fourth-round picks. Devin paid meaningful draft capital for present receiving strength; Dave accumulated future options. Bell is not on Dave's current roster at this snapshot, so the original return is not the same thing as today's lineup.
- **Jake received** Ashton Jeanty and Travon's 2027 twelfth-round pick. **Travon received** Trey McBride, Quentin Johnston, and Jake's 2027 eighth-round pick. Jeanty's first outing for Jake produced 26.70; McBride and Johnston combined for 17.20 in Travon's Week 1 lineup. That is one week's accounting, not a verdict on a multi-asset trade. Conveniently, these two owners meet in Week 2.

The in-week free-agent move in the captured feed was Rob adding Michael Mayer and dropping Kenyon Sadiq on September 12. With Brock Bowers already listed out, that is an actual tight-end contingency, not an acquisition to credit with someone else's points. Earlier waiver activity remains earlier activity.

## Injuries: What Changed, What Was Already There

**Confirmed Week 1 injury consequence:** Brown left New England's September 9 game and was placed on IR September 12. The team report says he must miss at least four games. His injury affected Michael's opening lineup; his absence now affects Rob's roster planning.

**Confirmed new concern:** Carolina's [September 14 report](https://www.panthers.com/news/monday-brew-dave-canales-addresses-run-defense-coker-on-precautionary-boot-and-more) says Coker rolled his ankle in the first quarter on September 13 and played through it. He described Monday's boot as precautionary and said he did not expect to miss time. That is encouraging player commentary, not medical clearance; Sleeper still marked him questionable in this snapshot.

**Existing absences:** the [official Week 1 report](https://www.nfl.com/injuries/league/2026/reg1) already listed Bowers out with a knee issue and TreVeyon Henderson out with an ankle issue. They should not be presented as newly injured during their games. Bowers belongs to Rob; Henderson belongs to Brian.

**Availability still to resolve:** Sleeper flags McConkey's chest, Jordan Mason's thumb, Alec Pierce's hand, and Brian Thomas' shoulder as questionable. Those tags identify concerns for Jake, Dave, Michael, and Travon respectively; they do not establish when each injury happened. Rob also has an out tag on Zay Flowers and a personal-absence designation on Josh Jacobs. Personal and coach's-decision absences are not injuries.
