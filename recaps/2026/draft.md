# 2026 Draft Recap — The League

_Every number here is taken from the league export for the 2026 draft: 240 picks, 25 keepers,
eight rosters of thirty. Production figures are 2025 points scored under this league's own
scoring, not a projection service. Market position is Sleeper's popularity-derived rank, which
is a rough proxy for where the field valued a player and is not a sourced ADP._

## The keepers come first

Nothing about this board makes sense until you know what was already on it. Twenty-five players
never reached the draft at all, and each one cost the round he is listed in. A keeper taken in
the ninth round is not a ninth-round bargain; he is a ninth-round pick already spent. All 25
were verified against the 2025 end-of-season rosters, and all 25 were on the same franchise
where the season ended.

| Owner | Keepers | Rounds spent | Combined 2025 PPG |
|---|:---:|---|---:|
| Brian | 4 | 11, 12, 13, 24 | 49.7 |
| Devin | 4 | 3, 13, 18, 20 | 46.0 |
| Rob | 3 | 9, 12, 24 | 43.3 |
| Jake | 4 | 11, 13, 14, 26 | 42.9 |
| Dave | 3 | 9, 10, 15 | 42.6 |
| Eric | 4 | 10, 12, 22, 24 | 40.4 |
| Michael | 3 | 14, 15, 18 | 33.9 |
| Travon | 0 | — | 0.0 |

Three teams kept three rather than four. Only one keeper slot in this league is ever taken away
— last place forfeits one — and in 2025 that was Dakota's team, which changed hands over the
winter. So Rob, Dave and Michael each kept three by choice, not by punishment, and each of them
walked into the draft with an extra live pick as a result.

Travon kept nobody. As an incoming owner he inherited the roster and none of its history, and
none of its keeper rights either. He drafted all thirty of his players. No one else drafted
more than 28.

## How the order was earned

The order runs roughly opposite to the 2025 final standings, with the champion picking last.

| Pick | Owner | 2025 finish |
|:---:|---|---|
| 1.01 | Eric | 5th — won the consolation final |
| 1.02 | Devin | 6th |
| 1.03 | Rob | 7th |
| 1.04 | Travon | 8th — franchise finished last, then changed hands |
| 1.05 | Brian | 3rd |
| 1.06 | Dave | 4th |
| 1.07 | Michael | runner-up |
| 1.08 | Jake | champion |

Eric's 1.01 is the most interesting pick in the league, because it was won rather than lost
into. Fifth place normally buys you nothing. Winning the consolation final bought him the first
overall selection, which is a rule worth remembering the next time a team's season dies in
November.

The one slot the finishes do not explain is 1.05 and 1.06: Brian finished third and Dave
fourth, but Brian picks fifth and Dave sixth. No first-round picks were traded, so it was not a
deal. Whatever settled it is not in the record.

At the other end, Jake drafts last, which is the tax on winning back-to-back titles.

## Rob bought the draft

This is the story of 2026, and it happened before a single pick was announced.

Rob acquired **seven** picks over the winter and gave up six. Look at what he bought and what
he paid:

- **Acquired:** a 2nd from Devin, a 2nd from Dave, a 5th from Michael, a 5th from Brian, a 6th
  from Dave, a 7th from Michael, a 12th from Jake
- **Gave up:** his own 5th, 13th, 15th, 18th, 22nd and 28th

He converted late-round lottery tickets into premium capital, and it showed immediately. Rob
made **three of the last six picks in round two** — 11, 14 and 15 — taking Derrick Henry,
De'Von Achane and Brock Bowers in the space of five selections. In a league that starts four
running backs and two flexes, that is not a stockpile. That is a starting lineup.

He came out of the night controlling 31 draft slots — tied with Jake for the most — and with the
highest projected starting lineup in the league by a distance.

## How the board actually went

**Round 1 was a running back round.** Seven of the eight opening picks were skill-position
runners or receivers. Eric took Jahmyr Gibbs first, Devin took Bijan Robinson second, and
Travon used the fourth pick on Jonathan Taylor — who, at 18.6 points per game in 2025, was the
most productive player available at the moment he was taken.

The exception was Dave at 1.06, who took Josh Allen. In a league that starts **two**
quarterbacks, spending a first-round pick on the position is defensible arithmetic rather than
a reach, and Dave was the only owner willing to do it. Michael followed at 2.10 with Lamar
Jackson.

**Then everyone waited, and then everyone panicked.** After Justin Herbert went at 3.21, the
quarterback position went quiet for eleven picks — and then broke open completely. Picks 33,
34, 36 and 37 were Joe Burrow, Dak Prescott, Patrick Mahomes and Caleb Williams: four starting
quarterbacks in a five-pick window. If you did not have two by the end of round five, you were
shopping in a cleared-out store.

**Jake ignored the run entirely.** Having kept four skill players, he took Jared Goff at 7.56
and Bo Nix at 8.57 on back-to-back picks and called it a quarterback room. It projects to 35.4
points a week, which is not the best in the league but is meaningfully cheaper than everyone
else's.

## The teams

Keeper value and draft value are judged separately below, because they were bought with
different money.

### Rob — Unstoppable Farce
**Keepers (3):** Jalen Hurts (R12, 18.7 PPG), Tyler Shough (R24, 17.9), Colston Loveland (R9, 6.7).
**Draft:** Ja'Marr Chase 1.03, then Henry, Achane and Bowers inside round two, Kenneth Walker,
George Pickens, Caleb Williams and Josh Jacobs.

The keeper haul is quietly outstanding: two starting quarterbacks held for a 12th and a 24th,
returning 18.7 and 17.9 points a game. That is what freed him to trade for offense everywhere
else. Loveland at a 9th is the one soft spot — 6.7 points a game does not justify a top-third
pick.

The draft is the best in the league by projection, and it is not close. **Projected starters:
213.6 points a week, 24.2 clear of second.**

### Dave — Early and out of hope
**Keepers (3):** Jaxon Smith-Njigba (R9, 14.2), Matthew Stafford (R15, 20.6), Emeka Egbuka (R10, 7.8).
**Draft:** Josh Allen 1.06, Jeremiyah Love, Cam Skattebo, D'Andre Swift, Rashee Rice, Sam LaPorta.

Stafford at a 15th is theft — 20.6 points a game for a pick most teams spend on a backup tight
end. Paired with Allen, Dave has the best quarterback room in the league at 43.4 points a week,
roughly three points clear of anyone else.

The bill comes due everywhere else. His projected starting four running backs average barely
ten points a game, Jeremiyah Love has no 2025 production to reason from at all, and eight other
players on the roster are in the same position. Dave has since traded Smith-Njigba to Devin,
which took the best of his three keepers off the roster. He still has the best quarterbacks in
the league and the most receiver production in any projected lineup, and he still projects last
— because nearly a third of his roster has no 2025 scoring line at all.

### Brian — Tri-Lams
**Keepers (4):** Jordan Love (R11, 15.7), Travis Etienne (R13, 12.8), Bucky Irving (R24, 10.8), Nico Collins (R12, 10.3).
**Draft:** Christian McCaffrey 1.05, CeeDee Lamb, Justin Herbert, Jaylen Waddle, DJ Moore,
TreVeyon Henderson.

The best keeper class in the league at 49.7 combined points, and the only one where all four
picks are defensible on their own terms — Irving at a 24th in particular. The draft that
followed is sturdy rather than thrilling: McCaffrey is a genuine anchor, and the Love/Herbert
pairing is solid.

The problem is the receivers. Brian rosters eleven wideouts and his best one projects at 10.3.
He has depth where the league is deepest and nothing that scares anyone.

### Eric — Sanders Boutte on Sunday
**Keepers (4):** Tucker Kraft (R10, 10.7), Chase Brown (R12, 12.6), Rico Dowdle (R24, 10.4), Blake Corum (R22, 6.8).
**Draft:** Jahmyr Gibbs 1.01, Drake London, Jayden Daniels, Tetairoa McMillan, Joe Burrow,
RJ Harvey, Jameson Williams.

Dowdle at a 24th is the sort of pick that wins a keeper league quietly. Kraft at a 10th gives
him a top-two tight end at a discount. Gibbs at 1.01 is the correct pick and needs no defense.

Eric did the hard thing well and the easy thing badly. The backfield is genuinely good — Gibbs,
Brown, Dowdle and Harvey project 49.4 across four starters. But he came away with the
lowest-projected quarterback pair in the league: Burrow and Daniels at 33.1 combined, in a
format that starts two every week. His WR4 projects at 8.3.

### Jake — Performance Anxiety
**Keepers (4):** Puka Nacua (R14, 15.4), Javonte Williams (R11, 13.0), Quentin Johnston (R26, 9.1), Luther Burden (R13, 5.4).
**Draft:** Saquon Barkley 1.08, Omarion Hampton, Trey McBride, Malik Nabers, Ladd McConkey,
Goff and Nix.

Nacua in the 14th round is one of the two best keepers in the league — a top-five market
receiver held for the price of a bench flier. Johnston at a 26th is nearly as good in relative
terms. Burden at a 13th is the miss; 5.4 points a game is bench production at a starter's
price.

The two-time champion drafted last, took the value in front of him, and punted quarterback to
rounds seven and eight. He has since sent McBride and Johnston to Travon for Ashton Jeanty, and
Keenan Allen to Michael, which leaves him with the worst tight end pair in the league. It
projects seventh. That is not an indictment — Jake won the 2025 title from a 7-8 regular season,
then won the two games that counted.

### Travon — Von
**Keepers:** none.
**Draft:** Jonathan Taylor 1.04, Ashton Jeanty, Chris Olave, David Montgomery, Patrick Mahomes,
Quinshon Judkins, Rome Odunze, Terry McLaurin, Brock Purdy.

Travon is the only owner who had to build a whole team in one night, and the right way to grade
him is against that task, not against rosters that started with four cornerstones already in
place.

He did it well. Taylor at 1.04 was the highest-scoring player on the board. Jeanty at 2.13 and
Judkins at 6.45 gave him a young backfield behind him. And while everyone else was fighting
over the quarterback run, he took Mahomes at 5.36 and came back for Purdy at 11.84 — the
second-best quarterback room in the league, assembled with a fifth and an eleventh.

Since the draft he has flipped Jeanty to Jake for Trey McBride and Quentin Johnston, trading
backfield depth he did not need for the tight end he did.

**He projects third, 25.4 points behind Rob and 1.2 behind second.** For a roster with zero
keepers, in a league where the other seven teams kept an average of 3.6 players, that is a
genuinely good night's work. The gap he still has to close is behind Taylor: his three other
starting running backs project 10.3, 8.4 and 6.1, and his flexes come in at 7.2 and 6.5.

### Michael — Cripple
**Keepers (3):** Drake Maye (R15, 20.6), Jake Ferguson (R14, 6.6), Ricky Pearsall (R18, 6.6).
**Draft:** James Cook 1.07, Lamar Jackson, A.J. Brown, Tyler Warren, Breece Hall, DeVonta Smith.

Maye at a 15th is arguably the best keeper value in the league — 20.6 points a game for a pick
most teams spend on a backup tight end — and pairing him with Jackson gives Michael a
quarterback room he barely paid for. The other two keepers returned 6.6 points a game each,
which is the weakest keeper class in the league by a clear margin at 33.9 combined.

Michael also controlled the fewest draft slots — 28 — after selling a 5th, a 7th and a 24th and
buying back only a 4th from Brian. The running backs behind Cook and Hall project at 6.4 each,
and in a league that starts four, that is where his season will be decided.

### Devin — Amon Another level
**Keepers (4):** Amon-Ra St. Brown (R13, 12.9), Jaxson Dart (R18, 18.6), Kyle Monangai (R20, 7.6), Justin Jefferson (R3, 6.9).
**Draft:** Bijan Robinson 1.02, Kyren Williams, Dak Prescott, Tee Higgins, Mike Evans,
J.K. Dobbins, Tony Pollard.

Dart at an 18th is superb, and St. Brown at a 13th is close behind. But the Jefferson keeper is
the most expensive decision anyone made this offseason: a **third-round** price for a player who
returned 6.9 points per game across a broken 2025. The verdict is already visible in the
lineup — Jefferson does not crack Devin's starting four receivers.

And yet Devin's roster projects **second** as it stands today, which is the quiet story of the
offseason. Bijan at 1.02 is unimpeachable, St. Brown is a real WR1, and Jaxon Smith-Njigba —
Dave's ninth-round keeper on draft night — has since arrived from Dave and now leads Devin's
receivers at 14.2. He assembled the league's second-best roster while his most expensive keeper
sat on the bench. If Jefferson returns to form, there is a gear here nobody has priced in.

## Steals and reaches

Keepers are excluded — a keeper is priced by the round he cost, not by where the market had
him. Late-round entries are also excluded, because a 27th-round pick landing 200 spots off
market position tells you nothing except that the shelves were empty.

Against market position, the picks that stand out inside the meaningful rounds:

| | Owner | Pick | Player | Market | Gap | 2025 PPG |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Taken earliest | Rob | 11.83 | MarShawn Lloyd (RB) | 136 | 53 early | — |
| | Travon | 12.93 | Jason Myers (K) | 124 | 31 early | 11.6 |
| | Michael | 12.90 | Cameron Dicker (K) | 119 | 29 early | 9.2 |
| Fell furthest | Devin | 23.178 | Dalton Schultz (TE) | 98 | 80 late | 5.4 |
| | Rob | 25.195 | Khalil Shakir (WR) | 120 | 75 late | 5.9 |
| | Brian | 28.222 | David Njoku (TE) | 131 | 91 late | 4.8 |

The honest summary is that this was a disciplined draft. Nobody made a catastrophic early
reach. The biggest falls all came after round 20, where market position stops meaning very
much, and the earliest selections against market were two kickers and a running back in rounds
eleven and twelve — which is to say, nothing that will decide a season. The decisions that will
actually decide 2026 were the keeper declarations and Rob's trades, both of which happened
months before anyone was on the clock.

## What the board says

One team clearly improved itself, and it did so by treating late-round picks as currency rather
than as lottery tickets. One team turned a consolation-bracket win into the first overall pick.
One team built from absolutely nothing, then traded its way to a third-place projection. And the
two-time
champion drafted last and took what was left, which by then was a quarterback room nobody else
wanted and the worst pair of tight ends on the board.
